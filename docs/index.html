Companies · ensemble

Ensemble = average of a margin score (operating margin compared with the same segment, its change over a year, and revenue growth) and a price score (6-month trend and calm, minus tail risk). All scores are ranks against the other companies here. "Aligned" = price and margins point the same way. "Diverging" = they disagree. This describes the latest data. It has not been back-tested, and it is not a recommendation to buy or sell.

Research: which fluctuations were worth trading?

Each rule's settings were picked on the older 60% of 10 years of history, then tested on the newer 40% it had never seen. Results are after costs.

How to read this. Returns are daily log returns, standardized to z = (r − μ) / σ. The normal curve is the bell curve with the same μ and σ. The cold-probability error is how often moves bigger than kσ really happened, minus how often the curve said they would. The quantum curve treats the return distribution as |ψ|² and expands ψ in harmonic-oscillator states; state 0 is exactly the normal curve, so ground-state purity measures how normal the market is and higher states hold the fat tails. The pulsing beam is those states evolving in time with phases e−i(n+½)t.

Losses are in-sample (the quantum curve is fitted to the same data it is scored on), so they describe the past, not the future. This is an analysis tool, not financial advice and not a price prediction.
